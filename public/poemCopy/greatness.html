"When does a translation of a poem become
an ekphrasis to that poem?" I ask Alexander,
dead on a slab in his Babylon palace. Quiet,
I trace the course of muscle and chiselled scars
across tanned skin with my untested fingers.
This corpse is cold already, like the marble
it will soon become. Such promise, waste.

"I think you are an ekphrasis of your father,"
I tell the man. "We share that, at least.
Behold our conquests, and shiver!" I tell
the drift of flies come fresh to the feast.
They at least will drown in honey as it floods
this man's gold cask. I dreamed of honey,
last night; I dreamed each heavy, glossy step
bringing me here to this Persian night, hot
still, parched air. I dreamed of desiccations.

"Why do you want to show me dead heroes?"
My father says nothing, mute in dead memories.
My invisible friend, stood behind, just smiles:
an old smile, a shiver of recall, his chest
still agape, his whisper drowned by its echo
in the presence of a warrior's nascent myths.
