She sits in stockings and sensible Christian
shoes, a height up to knees covered by a skirt
that smells old, stern, wrong. She is my Christmas,
this woman I must toddle towards, but she holds
no arms out to catch me. She has a face far above
and it wears my mother's lips, this woman who
is not my mother. There's a prickle tree, a battle
of pine against moth poison across my tongue
and within the sinus gap I can barely tickle
with upthrust fingers. The tree wears lights
to mark its entrance into my world: a wrongness
of outside brought to the room, like this woman
who makes my mother lose her love, refuse my need
to sit on her hip. Somebody above asks: what
do you think of the presents, child? All I see
is a jumble of boxes tumbled at the base
of the wronged spruce cut for this strange time's
own reason, bright colours around promises
yet to be kept. There's alcohol misting the air
in the room, there's a must of arguments
waiting to be unwrapped and Father Christmas
is an invisible man my invisible friend says
is a wrong I must say I believe, to make people
laugh.
