If death is no thing, what of rocks?
What of grave stones and tomb slabs?
No, they take shape; they set, direct.
The furnishings of cemeteries are things
that mean, yet death is no such thing.
Death is a shadow of sand, scraped words
that slump in tepid ripples. I stare
at our flat stretch of sea. Fresh air
is some thing good, you said: it heals
the soul. Here on the shore, your words
lie. I've never seen a soul; I've seen
lichen cuddled into that word on tombs.
You are not rock, not ash: you are salt
in water. A jolt of echoes in our skull.
