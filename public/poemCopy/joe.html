Joe is invisible, again. He's lowered
his hood to set his face free, let breathe
the scabs and growths in his bones
that dapple his jaw, his brow, his smile.

Joe knows he's invisible: the reflex lack
of eyes that look back – tell; the turn
of necks to shop displays – confirm:
the street is his toy to play with now.

As Joe takes steps, his majestic shadow
flutters ahead, clearing crowds so he can see
the artful measures taken to distill desires
to needs: firestorm sales to end all sales.

Look at what offers have been set to tempt
the coins in his pocket! For Joe has a stench
of many pockets, which he is eager to empty
and fill – though suited clerks can't spot Joe

beyond the tattooed knuckles holding coins,
ready to spin coppers across counters. Still,
a wealthy hand of uncaught thefts is as good as
a face that fits, for a man who cannot be seen.
