Who watches me? Is that you?
I see light frame a moment caught;
I feel a sunshine heat prick at skin;
A firestorm dries eyes, my face
in furrows. Do you feel me

or judge me? I want to be
tested, I think, or caressed
maybe. Seen; perhaps heard.
Who set the clouds to your face
to scud impossible skies?

And the digits on the device
in my hand shift. My eyes
fall to the chapel where fire
waits to burn. Old family, some
lost until found by your news, move

and together we queue to follow
the hired folks who slow-trolley
your box inside, and tied flowers,
and the jingle of a rat-pack song
and this isn't the end - you never

end. I wrote words to celebrate
your place in our shared air
and they shine on paper like hair
caught in sunshine – your light,
Mother: your firestorm of life.
