Who watches me? Can that be you?
I see quick light frame a moment caught;
I feel a stellar heat prick at my nape.
People wander and clump, twos and threes.
A sussur of gossips through late August air.

Can you smell me, taste me even?
I want to be tested, I think: seen;
heard; perhaps poked and caressed.
Who set the clouds to match your face,
let you scud across the blue dome sky?

And the digits that flick the device
in my hand shift. My stare settles on
the chapel where we shall shape the clay
of you and us for the bake. People, drawn
here by your news, catch signs of purpose

and together we queue to follow
the hired folks who slow-trolley
your tight box inside, and tied flowers,
and the soft jingle of a rat-pack song
and no this isn't the end - you never

end. I wrote words to celebrate
your place and worth in our shared air
and they shimmer over paper like hair
caught in sunshine – your light, mum:
your stroke to bring shine to the glaze.
