The apposite of radiance is – what? An absence?
A loss? A new black sun that consumes the ash and bone
of dead things and alchemists?

I would stare at the sun that casts its first light
through mists over the high Dymchurch tide, feel it
burn its black disc through my retina to the ancient
stem where the ticks of breath and pulse, fear and urge
surge like a flood's wave against a bone sea wall.
I cannot drown. I must learn: my shadow is a gift
of light; I am my shadow's shape.

Gold comes not from lead. Alchemists, like poets,
lie. Know that gold is forged in the churn of suns.
Are you yet consumed my brother, sister, mother?
Are you gilded in starlight, or moved beyond shape?

I would stare at the sun that casts its last light.
Another cycle of Dymchurch tide and still I stand here
on this wide wall that separates village from whale road.
I turn to meet the full moon, its rippled silver path
that cuts through cloud and sea mists to touch my boots.
I would step on that offer, ride each painted crest
to greet dead things and alchemists.

It is not my time. If I wait an hour more
I can sketch these words into sand and maybe
they will catch you in a place beyond know
and you will perhaps learn that even as I
strapped lead boots to our feet
I loved you all.
