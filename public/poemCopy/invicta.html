I don't have a happy place to aim this poem at.
People look weird. I'm sorry, but I had to type it.
When I look at a horse, I can see the horse;

I can see the point of the horse in the sweat
of its hock and lift of its hoof. I can admire
the sweep of the stallion in its shake of mane.
I love the way horse teeth extend from horse snout
to trim the tips of gorse. The Uffington horse
is a horse despite its limit of lines. That horse
has a destination, and the power to reach it
once it shakes the chalk from its green flank.

I was raised with the white horse of Kent embedded
in every space across the county. Even chairs.
My horse eternally balances on legs, like people.
As if it has reached its destination: my pride
which is the passion of peasants, not kings.

I look at people and I see spiders. I see claws
at the end of limbs; I see bright eyes and fat bellies
and sometimes I see spite wound tight in spun truth.
Even in Kent, my hill and chalk and bastard marsh.

Here is a happy place: a September web, sun-caught dew;
what mighty art we build, each alone in our skull.
