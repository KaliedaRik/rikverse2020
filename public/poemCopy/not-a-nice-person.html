I have a Box of Hugs. It came with a card,
some chocolate, condolence messages: we see
you, appreciate you. We want you to heal.

I've taken a moment to threaten the cynic
in me into a dark curve of my skull. I see
the message beyond the marketing; I'm hugged.

Today is the day for poppies. My cynic spits,
strapped to bone with meninges. There's flowers
still in bloom along road verges, in November.

Some of them are poppies: bent in their petals,
red against frost-burned grass. They refuse
the orders of planetary tilts; they persist.

I have many dead relatives to recall, this year:
a brother who ached to serve, but never served;
a mother who fled the threat of bombs over Kent.

I served, though not well. Seven weeks I stood
in blackened boots and cotton khaki. Some times
they paid me, like my father, my grandfathers.

"You don't need a rifle to be a hero." My cynic
is correct, of course. We can all be heroes,
but it takes a "brave will" and an "open heart",

and a willingness to commit plainspoken verse
without a lyric camouflage of images, sentiments:
all poets lie, mum, and some poems don't rhyme.

I have a Box of Hugs. I read the card and condolences,
enjoyed the chocolate. I've tucked some photos inside
its stout card space. I see room for a poppy, too.
