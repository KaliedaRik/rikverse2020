I had to steal my doll from a neighbour's bin.
A doll was required to complete my set of toys.
Toys were necessary to build out my first class.
I only drew on walls when witnessed by the class.
I learned the purpose of class from the television.
My father brought the magic box from the Outside.
I did not trust the Outside for it was wet, and cold.
My father did not approve of my theft of the doll.
My father approved of recycling, but not of dolls.
My father whispered: boys must not play with dolls.
I did not draw on walls when my father was present.
The doll was mostly clean, and wore a blue dress.
When I went Outside to a real school I drew on paper.
The doll then lived at the back of a dark cupboard.
The doll lived with a teddy bear with an orange eye.
I loved that bear; my name was not Christopher Robin.
My bear was called Bear, because that was its name.
My doll was called Doll; my train was called Train.
My father was called Dad, when I called him at all.
My invisible friend was tall: I called him Mister Man.
My invisible friend visited at strange times, and none.
My invisible friend whispered: he scared me a little.
I did not draw on walls in front of my invisible friend.
Mister Man was not part of my class. He was real.
It was never my fault that nobody else could see
the rag in Man's chest, his white-red hand.
