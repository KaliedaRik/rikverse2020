For every turn of the wheel
that I have endured, the world
is about to die. The end time
is nigh, my child, the wicked
shall spill from the halls
with blades and flames and we
must endure for the rapture
to come, or the worm to swallow
its tail or the wolf to rend –
a carnival ride's jaunty tune.

I do fear. The wonders of war
have turned away from the churn
of industrial gore to machines
that can target and blast flesh
with no bar set by human hand
or eye. Flesh is cheaper today.
The headlines call them drones,
these beasts that rip up borders
and pile into lives; an easy buy
for any gang that has the will.

When I was very young the world
was due to end in ice and empty
bellies and a scrap for the last
panda's pelt. Today we shall fry
or drown, perhaps both. A satire
of locusts waits on the margins
to strip me bare. I have an urge
to procreate each time I tap tap
the screen and scan the newslines,
such is the urgency of the words.

I should lay a bet on the cause
of my demise. I can get good odds
on a chest infection. The growth
of tumours is not as terminal
as it used to be, and a good stent
can solve many heartbreaks. Kerbs
remain a potent threat. I've grown
so good at mouth-clap apocalypse
that I need to relearn the art of
little deaths, the ones that hurt.
