I fret about next door. There's noises
I can trace through walls: a tap on brick,
a whine through pipe; a voice of maybe
words, or hymns. A chime of stuttered laugh.

It can't be rats, or squirrels. Poison
grains sown across the roof tiles trap
such shelter seekers, their flesh a soft
dissolve of rots into the mossed gutters.

I lift my ear from wallpaper, edge on toes
to my tight bathroom for water and release
of water. I watched the family move out
months before, after the vicar visited.

I do not mind the noises. They echo like
old friends one welcome away and still
I won't miss them when the roof folds in.
A pigeon pecks at slates, rolls, falls.
