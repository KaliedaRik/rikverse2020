I fret about next door. There's noises
I can trace through walls: a tap on tile,
a whine through pipe; a murmur of maybe
words, or hymns. A stutter of low laugh.

It can't be rats, or squirrels. Grains
of poison sown over the roof slates trap
such shelter seekers, their flesh a soft
dissolve of rots slithered to mossed gutter.

I ease my ear from wallpaper, edge on toes
to my tight bathroom for water and release
of water. I watched as the family moved out
months before, after the stockinged vicar came.

I do not mind neighbours. They echo like
old friends one welcome away and still
I won't miss them when that roof folds in.
A pigeon pecks at pellets, rolls, falls.
