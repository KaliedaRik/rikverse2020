It's not often the corpse doesn't turn up
for its own funeral. The crowd that gathers
by the church gate is younger, this time.
I don't know them: not my generation. Most
have made an effort to dress up, not used
to such terminal celebrations, not sure
of the protocols. My sister-in-law dresses
in a warm cardigan, shabby like me, inured
to the judging looks: "I still struggle," she says.
"The house is too quiet, too cold now he's gone."
We exchange catch-up words: me, her, her daughter
who has yet to rummage through her Nan's jewels
stashed in the box I brought to the event.

"It's all Paul's fault," I tell the gravestone.
"He started this race seven months ago and then
everyone else joined in. You must be busy
up there, Dad. Is he speaking to you yet?
Is there forgiveness in Heaven today?" The crowd
got too busy for me. I swapped their milling
confusion for a walk past the old yew to find
this spot. Dad's pot lies buried in his Dad's bed
next to his son's too-small box of ashes - Alan
I still miss, eighteen years now. The stone shows
the right name, but the wrong dates; Grandad
knew a thing about saving pennies when he insisted
Dad shared his name. Mum put a stop to it,
dividing the names between my last-living brother
and me. His first and second our unspoken middles.

"They did a good job for Gran," says Andy
beside me now. Two plots to the left I see
the gaudy new marble, clean letters for Aunt Olive
and her Mum who got buried in the wrong earth
due to some idiot planting a stranger in the space
she paid for. "Maybe we should do something
like that for Alan and Dad?" I suggest. "We know
where they are, ain't that enough?" He's right,
of course. "I'll show you where they planted
uncle John," he finishes, and leads me to a dip
in the field that only he can see. "Don't stand
on him!" I give him a cool glance: "Is uncle Len
anywhere around here? I could do with a piss!"

We walk back to the church, together for once.
The crowd grows restless, and the Vicar charges
by the hour. It's not our problem: this relative
has his own family dynamics, which is why, I suppose,
he gets two funerals. My nephew died a year younger
than Alan. He had plans to drive to my Mother's burning
but got taken elsewhere by his heart that morning.
His mother died last week. Another cancer.
I'll miss you, sister. I can already hear you
and cousin Avril (pneumonia, Good Friday)
cackling like witches at the chaos below.
