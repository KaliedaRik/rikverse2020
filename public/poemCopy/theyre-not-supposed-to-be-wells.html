They're supposed to be people. I can see
the shape of a person by the shadow it grows
and the air it expels. Wells drink air
like I sup tobacco. They hide their shapes
in holes and leap at me as I wander by.
I struggle to trust the offer they make.

They're supposed to be faces. I can see
the shape of a face by the calderas it builds
between the lip of the brow and the ridge
of the nose. Deep they delve, like wells,
and fill with a bubble of magmas burping
sulphur over skin: dead skin; stone skin.

They're supposed to be graves. I can see
the shape of a grave by the tears it springs.
My eyes are fur, see? You can poke a finger
into my socket and stroke me towards calm
if you want. Can you feel the heat I keep
at the long bottom of me, marble in shadow?
