Science is certain that when life ends
death results. We cease; I cease. All stops.

Like the time between me waking in fear
at four, then blinking into half seven.

That's not the petite mort the French wink at:
that's a different moment; this is mere loss.

I can live with the idea of vacant sense.
It's the approach I fear: the pain; the wait.

How will I die? My mother shrank into old
beyond decency, reduced to a dozen memories.

Cousin Avril feared her heart, yet ended
drowned by pneumonia. I still smoke.

My siblings bookended the year with slow slump,
mistle tumors suckling blood from good flesh.

I wonder if my nephew woke in fear at four
then just forgot to end his blink?

Death is not the end, the bible says.
I am to be judged, though I lack the belief.

I like the idea of tunneling into
a fresh womb, ride the galactic wheel

that never stops. I prefer the grace
of the palace beyond. My library awaits.

Too many deaths this year, I tell
the shadow sat on my chest and me caught

as a corpse in the depth of my bed – no.
I blink this night's terror to dust.
