I thought of writing two poems, today:
When We Were Very Buff; and Now We Are Fat.
It makes my brow flex, these invitations.
They offer such marvellous avenues
into my kingdoms of nostalgia, regret.

Evangelists on the prowl entice me:
accept Jesus into your life, let Him
fix you. They appear in my video feed
soft, enhanced. Confess for chance: a key
to the gates of Heaven, salvation.

A wind outside knocking my door: bang.
And some moments, bang. The wind needs
to concentrate on its work to strip
leaves from trees, trees from soil.
Bang. Easier to remove the knocker.

I used to be buff, toned, carved smooth
in sweat and effort, purpose, glory.
After each training, a cigarette.
I love the feel of my body, the way
it flexes, bends, now that I'm fat.

I've never liked autumn, mum. I practised
my new curse words when you told me to wrap
up tight and play outside with wet leaves.
Dead stuff can fuck off! I don't need it
wrapping me tight in nostalgia, regret.

YouTube fatigues me; I rummage for storms
on Wikipedia. There was a gale back in
twelve eighty seven that sunk the Ports
and fixed the course of a river, history.
Church bells bang brine: now that's a poem.
