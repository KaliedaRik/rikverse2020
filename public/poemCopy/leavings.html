I have taken up crayons and cast my will
onto the stripped walls of my current home.

Events, some unfortunate, have brought me
to consider the shapes of each letter I draw.

My skin I shall leave to the man who begs
in Leytonstone; it will lighten his hunger.

My feet I bequeathe to the fox who took
old dolls to hide in her busy garden den.

I enjoy the way the wax in my hand
colours over cracks in the unpainted wall.

I have so many more poems to distribute
but they lack substance; they fill no belly.

My breath I shall give to Jesus, or maybe
Aten, whichever has the will to fight for it.

I've buried my dreams in caskets across
the Hackney Marshes, in case I return as a crow.

My curses are reserved for the cunts who
sell my home beneath me, to balance spreadsheets.

Do you like my decisions? I ask my lover.
They need more glitter, he replies with a kiss.

My family stole my sparkle, I tell him,
returning his hug. I let each death take more.

But most I've saved for you, like this crayon.
Let's witness my last testament together, yes?
