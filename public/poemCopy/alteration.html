I have wool; I have thread. I have
hooks and needles to create shapes
that lie flat on a workbench. Wings
can be flat, though an angel
with knitted wings is nothing:
a Christmas spruce topper to spin
the whitest of festive lies.

You unpicked my favourite jumpers
and made me kneel in front of you
with hands up and apart as you wound
their shades of sprung wool to a skein.
And then you made me a fresh jumper
but it lacked the pattern I liked.
I lost my new gloves that winter:
a deliberate act to prove no chill
air would ever be the master of me.

So, shall I crochet some angel wings
to sew to my recollections of you?
It is a tempting cliche; I have wool.
