Death isn't an end. No, death
is that time when a storm
decides to rearrange your head
into new shapes and unfurl
a churned map for your life
to follow. Like tuning a radio
to static where once song
and gossip could be found.

The torrent at the bus stop, beyond the hospital
where we exchanged our last touches, woke me wide.

Death is words in your thoughts
that jelly like wet sand, meanings
unhitched from dictionaries,
messed signs that sharpen joy
and blunt anger. Fresh creases
on a rescued map of a life
now as inert as missed meetings.

You cancelled our next meeting through a refusal
to keep breathing. I respect your reasons. I hate them.

I can see your smile, your eyes,
but the lines of your face are as gone
as the old postcard of you leading donkeys
along Dymchurch beach.
