Death: a strange season, a storm
that bangs in, to jazzle histories
into new, tide-washed-sand tracks.
A different map for twined life
to follow; a seventies trannie
tuned to static where once song
and gossip could be enjoyed.

The torrent at the bus stop, beyond the hospital
where we exchanged our last touches, woke me wide.

Death: an edit of recalled instants
that stick like wet sand, moments
scratched clear of their frames
to sharpen joy and blunt anger.
A net of fresh-cut symbols over
long-stored directions to a life
now as inert as a missed meeting.

You cancelled our next meeting through a refusal
to keep breathing. I respect your reasons. I hate them.

I can see your smile, your eyes,
but the lines of your face are as gone
as the old postcard of you leading donkeys
along Dymchurch beach.
