So there's this bloke I know. Well, I say I know him: we've been sharing cigarettes on our doorsteps for years. You get to know someone when you're both shivering for nicotine on a cold night in February!

Let's call him Ben, this bloke, because Ben is a nice, common name. He says he used to have an uncle called Ben but he died a long while back – though that Ben survived a strangled testicle which turned to the gangrene and had to be removed so that version of Ben was then known as One Bollock Benny and that's all we need to know about him.

My mate Ben, he's had a bit of a year. Well, he's been having quite a few of these 'bits of a years' lately. His partner has not been well. Lung problems, mainly, but also joint problems, mobility problems. Ben is in his early sixties and his partner is a little bit older, recently got a state pension. Also got a diagnosis of pulmonary fibrosis, but not the aggressive form. Ben does what he can to support his partner – they've been together for over thirty years and he says the 'in sickness' thing is part of the contract so he's happy to support his best friend whenever that help is called for.

Of course, it means the more basic pleasures of the relationship don't get catered for – hasn't been looked after for quite a few years now. Not that I take an interest! The man hints he's happy to sort himself out: good friendship is more important to him than quibbling about a lack of relief. They still share a bed, after all – it's all good and nice, but I prefer to chat about other things when we're out checking the lawn for dandelions.

Ben and his partner live in social housing. Their landlord forgot they existed for a while, let the maintenance of the property slip. Which is annoying for the house prices, but it's not the man's fault. Recently the housing association told them that they wanted to sell the property because the rent being paid doesn't come near to covering the costs of maintaining the place. Like I said, they've been together for thirty years in that place, and they don't seem keen on moving somewhere else. Ben does try to put a positive spin on things – it might be an opportunity in disguise – but you can see that the thought of moving weighs in his eyes, sometimes.

Anyway. Ben's bit of a year, yeah? His mum just died. Last week, in fact. She was ninety six, almost ninety seven – which is a ripe age! Ben told me he was relieved when his brother phoned with the news. Said him and his partner had been down to the coast to visit her the day before she passed. Said it was like she summoned them, because he had been planning to go down the week before but put it off. That last visit, it was pretty clear, pretty quick, that his mum was ready to say goodbye, which they managed to do even though the woman was past the stage of talking. She was in a huge amount of pain so mostly they held her hand, chatted to her, asked her questions and watched for the tiny head nod, or tiny head shake. Small stuff, yeah? She was still in there, he said. Ninety seven, almost!

Told me he did cry the morning he heard of her passing, but I reckon I caught a tear the evening before when they got back to London, when he described the way she had reached her hand up and rested it across his chin for a good five minutes – that's the bit that near cracked him, I reckon.

Mind you, that was not a normal relationship, that one. Ben's the youngest of five kids, see, and born nine years after the others. He's been hinting that he wasn't enjoying those visits back home. They weren't in-and-out visits, he admitted. Often he'd sit outside the care home, along the canal bank nearby, just gearing himself up to walk through the door. I remember his mum well: a very independent woman, very active until her knees and hips got the better of her. She got rushed to hospital at the start of October last year – deep vein thrombosis, with a clot travelling up to her lung.

Ben was on holiday in Greece at the time – their first holiday abroad in six years, since before Covid, but he didn't get the first plane back. We were house-sitting the cats for them, so there wasn't any need on my part. After she was admitted, she refused point blank to get out of bed. Never did, either, Ben said, from that day to her last. Drove poor Ben up the wall because his mum hated losing her independence; resented it, got nasty about it.

Ben's brother Gavin had to take most of that venom over the past fifteen years – she lived with him in the house where Ben was born. Gav bought the house with his mum and brother Dave decades back, before buying them out so he owned it all.

Yeah, there's something about that house business. But the man don't tell me everything.

I do know Gav survived bladder cancer. He got the diagnosis during Covid, put up with the treatments for a year before finally persuading the surgeons to just cut half his guts out. He's been in remission since then, even sewed the little bags he uses to carry his piss bag in – Ben laughed about that because Gav's always been a navvy. Gav also seems to be handling most of the funeral arrangements, while Ben has done some of the messaging on Facebook – you know, contacting grandkids, posting announcements. Gav's asked him to write a eulogy – Ben does have a way with words – but I don't know how that's coming on.

He is lucky, is Ben. When he told his work about his mum dying they immediately told him to take at least a week off work. He works for one of those woke outfits which takes the mental health of their employees seriously. He thinks it's all a bit stupid. His boss told him to go away and look after himself when he logged on last Tuesday. I think he's worried people will think he's taking the piss.

Mind, he didn't take more than a day off work when his eldest brother Jack died in February. He really didn't like his eldest brother, said he barely knew the man – there was a fourteen year gap between them. He used to babysit the nephews and nieces when he was a teenager but has barely had anything to do with the family since he came to London. You know, after he quit the army back in '89. Ben didn't like the way the man would scream at his kids. But maybe also because the man was a bit anti-gayer? By the sounds of it, his big brother was the sort of bloke who'd parrot whatever the Sun or Mail suggested. Ben told me he sent Jacky a birthday card and a Christmas card every year, signed from him and his partner. Mainly out of spite.

How did Jacky go? Oh, cancer. Two years with lumps in his throat. A bit unexpected as by all the things Ben said, the man was responding quite well to the experimental treatments they were giving him. Ben told his bosses about it when Jacky died, but flatly refused to take any time off. The only day he did take off was for the funeral: took his laptop down to the care home so he could sit with his mum to watch the livestream. You know, he said there was no mention of anyone outside his brother's family and friends through the entire service. Not even his brothers and sister, or his mum. Then again, Jacky only visited his mum on Mother's Day and Christmas Eve – even though he only lived five miles down the road. Weird family all round, if you ask me!

What? His sister? She's had a life, too! Lives in Birmingham now. Moved up there while she was in the women's refuge system, back in '02. Janey has five kids of her own – three different fathers, abandoned four of them back in the day to get out of a different bad marriage and move to London, then had the fifth there. Ben used to be very close to her. He lived with her in a squat – well, an illegal sub-let – for a couple of years after he came out of the army. But they kinda lost contact when she moved north. Thinking about it, the whole family is a bit crap when it comes to keeping in touch.

He's been saying that Janey's been having problems. Told him she had cancer in her hip, when they were on the phone about Jacky's death. She's tried the radiation but decided she didn't like it, was refusing more treatment. He went up to visit her a couple of months ago. Told me he didn't recognise her when they met in the station. She was shrunken and shrivelled, bad hair cut, walking really slow. They had a chat and a burger. She was swigging that oromorph stuff out of a bottle, for her pain, and had some other little pills too. She wouldn't let him hug her hello or goodbye, because of the pain, Ben said. I think that shook him up.

He told me a funny story about Janey, though I don't think it's funny. He was trying to get hold of her to tell her the news about their mum, last week when she died, but she wasn't picking up. In the end he had to call her youngest kid. Turns out that while he'd been visiting their mum the previous day, Janey had taken a tumble in the street, and the reason why she wasn't answering was because she was in the operating theatre getting an emergency new hip! I did ask Ben this morning how she was getting on and he said she was still in hospital – which sounds wrong to me because they usually send new hipsters home as soon as possible after the op. Can't be taking any chances with all them hospital superbugs floating around, I suppose. He did look quietly suspicious, as if his sister ain't telling him the full story. But it's not my place to pry too deep, you know.

That reminds me! Another one to add to the list is his cousin Dee. She passed on Good Friday, which Ben says was a shock. Dee had had a pacemaker fitted for her heart a few weeks before and was by all accounts doing really well. Then she got rushed into hospital all of a sudden. Didn't make it. Pneumonia, of all things! I know Ben and Dee and Janey were all really close back in the day. Thick as thieves by all accounts. Ben didn't make Dee's funeral because nobody told him, or his mum, when it was happening. Communications in that family are just buggered, like I said. Ben said he got angry instead and went down the football pitches, swore at some trees, the day he found out Dee was already burned. Really funny woman too, that Dee. Funny ha ha, that is.

What's that, you say? Was he close to his mum? Well yes, but then again no. He told me the first feeling he had when Gav phoned him with the news was – yeah, that's it: relief! You know, relief that her suffering was finally over, that she was in a better place with her dad and Dave. Who's Dave? Oh, he's the fifth kid – well, fourth kid – died back in '09, which really upset the mum. Though I remember at the time Ben told me he'd shared a bedroom with Dave for over two decades, but he didn't know how to cry for him when he passed. That one was a heart attack on an army shooting range – he was one of the caretakers up there. Died on his lunch break, bird watching. If you're going to go, Ben said, then that seems like as good a way as any!

But Ben and his mum. Funny relationship. I think Ben, being the youngest and so far behind the others, I think he felt a bit smothered by his mum when he was a kid. I know he had a bad time of it when his mum and dad got divorced. Ben told me things got a bit hairy and he was sleeping on people's sofas for a few months before he moved back in with his dad. Then his mum moved back in too and everything went silent for a while. He found out later that a high court judge made him a ward of the court – whatever that is – then refused to give custody to either his mum or dad when the divorce was finalised. Made him a fucking adult, Ben said. Apparently the judge told his mum to give Ben the child benefit money, but he just told her to keep it – he called it his rent money. I'd have kept the money, personally, but I weren't there so what do I know!

He'd only just turned sixteen when that happened. After that ... I don't know. It took him four years to finally get out the house: joined the army; left the army; moved to London. For a long while he did all he could to minimise the visits back home, but he always seemed to be on good terms with his mum. She'd come and stay with him and his partner maybe a couple of times a year? At least until her joints stopped the visits – in the end she struggled with getting up the stairs to their flat.

Ben's dad? Oh, he was gone not long after the divorce. I think Ben was seventeen by then. That was cancer, too: a quick one, Ben told me.

But yeah, Ben didn't like going back home. Especially this last year, though he's been travelling to various bits of the county every two to three weeks since he got back from Greece. His poor mum was being shoved from pillar to post those first few months, before they finally got her into a nursing home not far from where she lived.

Ben told me that he was getting fed up with the number of skeletons falling out of cupboards each time he visited. Each time, another new revelation or two. Apparently his mum got a bit more honest over those last few months – though he did wonder about some of the things she was saying: the senility was beginning to kick in quite hard by then. He said that the last few visits had been a bit easier because she was down to remembering just a few life stories, and none of the hard ones.

I remember one time he came back looking a bit shocked. He said his mum told him a story about how she had named him after her older brother, who had died when he was six weeks old. He said he always thought he had been called – well, not Ben, that's just what I'm calling him here – she'd given him his name because she liked the sound of it. Never heard of this dead sibling before.

I know the visits over the last few months had been taking it out of him. He told me that, for some reason, his mum would start going on about why he had been born. To 'save the marriage', she kept saying, over and over. Ben told me it was old news to him – something he learned long before his parents divorced. But what shook him up was the look of hatred in his mum's face whenever she mentioned it. The woman despised his dad. Hated him for talking her round.

Yeah, he didn't seem so eager to talk about that. Don't sound healthy to me at all, but at least none of them went for the shotguns. What's that? Oh yeah, they had guns in the house, Ben told me. Part of the rural life. Can you believe it?

Anyway, he's certainly been through the mangle several times, this past year, has my mate Ben.

I do hope he's okay!
