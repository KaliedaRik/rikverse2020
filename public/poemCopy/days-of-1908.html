(After Μέρες του 1908 by Κωνσταντίνος Καβάφης)

That year he found himself without work:
He lived off the cards,
the backgammon, recycled day-loans.

Work was offered, three pounds a month,
attending to needs in a stationery store.
He had refused it without hesitation.
He didn't chase it: a salary was not sought,
young with sufficient learning, twenty-five years old.

Two, maybe three shillings a day he earned; he wasn't winning.
The things a lad learns from playing cards and backgammon,
in the neighbourhood cafes, the popular ones:
no matter how smart he played, poor chances were waiting.
The loans, they came and remained.
Rarely was the pound found, more often half,
once it scraped down to the shilling.

Every few weeks, sometimes more,
as if fleeing from a torture of a sleepless night,
he cooled off in the baths, a morning swim.

His garb was a frayed mess.
He wore the same outfit all the time,
cinnamon faded clothes.

A summer day in nineteen oh eight:
an image caught and kept, a rightful pleasure,
the patch-bleached cinnamon cloth gone.

That caught-kept memory protected him
from when he was browning, when he was shedding
his sweat-worn shirt, his patched undercloth.
And he remained naked: complete, impeccable; a miracle.
His hair lifted up and combed;
his limbs a little sunburned
from the morning nudity in the baths, on the beach.
