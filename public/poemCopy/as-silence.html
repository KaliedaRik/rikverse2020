We watch as the celebrant calls in the quiet.
Please take these moments to reflect on how
my brother enhanced our lives, he invites.

"He got a good crowd," I say to my mother
lying at a slope in her care home bed.
She keeps her eyes on the livestream, silent.

I reach over and take her hand. Paper skin
is supposed to be dry. She grips fingers over
my thumb: "Two down; two to go," she announces.

I stare at her, half-grinned, half lost.
"There's five of us, mum," I remind her.
"I was only counting the boys," she finishes.

I return to silence; I know my place. March
blusters through the part cracked windows.
Her fingers are too chill, her smile fixed.
