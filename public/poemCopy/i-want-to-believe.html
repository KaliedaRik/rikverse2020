There's a cat dead in the canal. Thin mist
can't hide its mat of weed-stained fur, tawn
over white. A young coot, free from the beaks
of killer parents, picks at the strings of food
presented by this unexpected arrival.

Perhaps the feline lost its footing as it chased
a vole over the bank and into the air. The canal
offers no ladders for small swimmers to find.
Everything happens for a reason, I lie to myself.
Better to put trust in my matted imagination
than wallow in clear contemplation: cats in bags.

You're in a happier place now, I tell the corpse.
The fib worked for me when I whispered it here
a few months back, fresh from that expected,
dreaded text on my phone. I should have drowned
that device when I had the chance. If I lived feral
I could live happy, deaf to all chatter beyond
these tawn trees. Near my cat, near hidden in mist,
a circle of green feathers, a parrot's epitaph.

I feel feral, this morning. I can play at ghosts
in this mist, its promise to smooth the world,
to sweeten the chatter that soaks my mobile life.
