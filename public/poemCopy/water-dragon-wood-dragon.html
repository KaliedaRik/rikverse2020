Because my now is the twenty twenties
and my how is a lightfall of video
and if we don't capture actions
we don't exist. You don't exist.

I lost my photos of us in a crash
of uncopied disks. You were backed up
in clouds until my plastic expired:
we don't exist. You don't exist.

There's memories. You laughed the length
of Leytonstone High Road that time
I shopping trolleyed you home, drunk.
That time someone stole our front door –

we don't exist? You don't exist?
What if my sounds and sights of you
are just lies I conjure for pleasure?
A life without tears is a tissue, dissolved,

and we don't exist – you don't exist –
and the only image of you I possess
is a kid in his duffel jacket,
his big, big sister knelt by his side

and I know we posed though my recall
of that now, that how, slipped away
a child's grin after the camera snapped
light into shadows into paper, thin framed.
