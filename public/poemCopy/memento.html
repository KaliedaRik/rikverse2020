Mausoleums irk me. Pyramids on the horizon struck
better at sunset as I stood amid Cairo minarets.

The best tombs I've stood before settle in Havana.
Lalique pours radiance amid shroud-capped pillars.

Perhaps a graven message to honour a life? No,
I lack the mason's chisel eye to find good stone.

An arch, rough-wrought in wood, yellow in its lichen
pelt, a feast of ruby hips on clamber-rose boughs:
I thought I'd find you here, mum.
