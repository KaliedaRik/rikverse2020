In the window she sees clouds
ahead of the moon and the faces of sweethearts,
their eyes rain on the pane.

Her eyes are bright, trapped
in a prison of paper skin - so easy to tear
the lies of lines with a nail.

They lie, too. They lie when They spoon
tepid food through dry lips. The taste she aches
to feel on her tongue is rich with heat.

Her gran must have hugged her,
left her smell in the room; it clings like ghosts
to her hair, her hands, her throat.

"Alice, dear. It's time for bed."
Hands take her shoulders, push bones to blankets.
A flat screen of colours dies.

"Allie, Allie!" This voice, she knows.
She can make out his face past the window bars,
his smile. A womb's press on her bladder.

They lie, too. They lie when They scrape
damp cloth over places only her Archie can touch
in the summer shimmers under their tree.

Such a long trek to reach the chair:
the flu's been bad this year, it's journeyed so far
from Hong Kong to steal her strength.

She sits in her room: a chair, a bed,
four walls, a door, a night of sky. No words anymore
except for her face in the moon.
