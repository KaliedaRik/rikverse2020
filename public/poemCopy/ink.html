I love the way my wicked cat
walks the hallway, winding her tail
upright to greet me: are you all good?
Is food in my future? I can find the bowl
to help you reach the right answer.

I'm busy, cat. Can you not see
me staring hard at the head of my shower?
It accuses me: it keeps a tally
of my washless hours – perhaps a week? –
from its needle waters: I can knead the ache
of muscled knots, it nags at me,
I can wash your sins from skin and mind
and burn away your wayward grief.

Must I bicker with bathroom fittings?
Is that the range of my thoughts today?

I need to mark my mother in skin.
She adored elephants; I could draw a trunk
to an anchor's knot and ask the beast
to haul it across the crease of my back.
Perhaps it could stamp on the stems of roses?
She liked her roses, I reason to the cat
now sat at stare: a spider, perhaps?
