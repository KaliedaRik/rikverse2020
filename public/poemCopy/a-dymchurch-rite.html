I'm lying when I tell my friends
the church doors blew their hinges when
the priest cast water on my head.
How can an eight day babe enrage
a God who welcomes sinners home
to Heaven? Such fools I have for friends!

But hear me out, for sometimes lies
are japes and jokes can hide a truth
that hurts. They stole my thrupence coin,
one time, at Sunday School: they claimed
that Jesus needed cash to heal
the world. I needed coins for sweets.

Mum needed peace at home; she dreamed
to see me dressed in choral cloth,
my voice a congregation's gift:
my throat's a seal's performance, mum,
and God deserves a better song
than mine. I'm glad to fail you both.

And there his image, propped on wood.
My nephew gone before his time:
a sleeping death, an unfixed valve
that stopped his heart and brought me here.
I shift my gaze and spot the font:
well met again, old friend, it says.
