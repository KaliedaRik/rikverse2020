I cannot sing for you, my cat:
my throat is poorly shaped for song
and words get stuck inside my throat
and melodies stay furled in lungs.

I cannot sing for you, my cat.
Your voice was rarely raised; I thought
you were a statue kept beside
the fridge, too scared to move or call.

I cannot sing for you, my cat,
though you have earned a song from me
to tell of origins in bags
disposed in bins: unwanted trash.

I cannot sing your grace in life,
of moments caught like flies in claws
when warm rays rouged your fur to shine
and love-blinks shared within our stares.

For you a quiet fuss sufficed:
a loop of string; a flash of wing;
a rub of ear when serving food;
my shoulder offered for your perch.

No need to entertain your hosts,
these two-legged freaks who rescued you
and asked no more of you than just
be you, our cat, and sometimes purr.
