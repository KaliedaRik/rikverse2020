The knots in this recall are a bramble:
is the fruit worth the scratch? I see
a room – dusty, musty – but I worry
the walls are built from your words,
not my eyes, my easy supple synapses.

The knots around your mouth? They were
a truth, I know, because I witnessed them
many times, most often when you thought
of her, of you in her life, her rooms.
Her thorny fingers directing your chores.

The knots in this wall are simple: a cross,
heavy in its wood, lacking that salvation
corpse. Black, hung from wire, or maybe
string. My eyes would have been young,
that visit I might have made to that room.

I remember your reaction, or maybe a report
of your reflex when you lifted the cross
from its hook. It burned you; you threw it
across the space. When I picked it back up
I felt no sting – a knot of false memory?

If I entered that room, it was a few days
past the death of your mother. I have
one fast memory of that knot of a woman
and a wealth of anecdotes: tambourines
and determined street worship in cold air.

Your puckered distaste for her held no knots.
In this, at least, you were pure, much as
the refusal of her favourite cross to let
you caress its touch worn varnish was a truth
you kept safe in your thoughts to the last.

The knots in this recall are a bramble,
but sometimes the provenance holds no weight.
If I bore my grandmother's cross for a moment
… it doesn't matter. You wore her scratches
like a martyr's crown: that is enough, yes?
