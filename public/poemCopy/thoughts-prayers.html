Soon, I shall perform grief. Organisers have determined
that attendees should comply with a formal regime of ties
and suits of sober cloth; I am permitted to accessorise
with the deceased's beloved football team's attire.
I stare at my heap of rumpled fabrics: I possess neither
jacket nor iron; I lack a sober noose to knot to my throat.
I have black boots with black laces - will that suffice?

Bedbugs have invaded my office space and physical presence
has been suspended until after the poisons clear. Still,
I must perform the signage appropriate to the formality
of each meeting: dry eyes and sad smiles when offered
condolences for my latest loss. They accumulate like a tally
across the columns of a spreadsheet, five now. The firm
is kind: that I take time to accommodate this new situation,
that I seek counsel, that I sit and listen with sympathy
to people similarly struck by terminal adversities. Stories
heal when shared. In our safe space we shall perform grief.

How can I tell the horror-thoughts that eat me each night?
How can I share the conviction that my situation
is a fucking joke and I'm not yet laughing?
That praythoughts don't help? My ears block
stories, phrases. My throat stops words
beyond rote, reflex deprecations.
I can see the fat-stick paint
of the sad clown society
wills onto my face
and I spit!
Spit!
