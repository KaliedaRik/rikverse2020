I sit on the eve of the day of the dead
and wish for normal things: a walk; a drink;
a good meal smoothed with bread and thin gravy.

Let them come, if they dare. Let them dance
between the seams of my underdressed dreams.
Already my know-crow calls to familiar threads.

I would be done with my dead. They demand
too much: thought, hurt, time. I have a box,
a good box of wood in which to wind their mist.

I walk the short length of my fresh-painted hall
with new-laundered shirts hung from my arms,
one for each mirror to welcome clean sleep.
