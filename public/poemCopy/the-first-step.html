(After Το Πρώτο Σκαλί by Κωνσταντίνος Καβάφης)

Young Eumenes was complaining
one day, to the poet Theocritus:
"I've been writing for two years now
and I've completed just one idyll.
It's the only proper thing I've finished.
It belittles me to see how high
– so tall – the poetry ladder is,
and from this first rung where I stand
I see no way to go up. It drains me of joy."

And Theocritus said: "These words of yours
are stray words, blasphemous even.
If you know you're on the first step
then you must be proud and happy.
This step where you stand is no little thing;
it is a glorious step you have taken.
You know, even this first solid step
is far beyond the crowds below.
For just climbing onto the ladder
earns you the right to know that you are
a citizen in the City of Ideas.
It is difficult to step into the city
and rare indeed to be claimed by it.
In that city's Agora sits a host of lawmakers
who see through all but the true adventurer.
Here's where you've arrived; it is not little:
what you've achieved deserves its glory!"
