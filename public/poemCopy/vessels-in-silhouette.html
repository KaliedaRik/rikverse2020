I am not a small boat for a random group of selfish
genes, mum. Crossing the Channel in choppy seas
is not the point of me, neither am I forced to wear
inheritance without complaint: I can change me.

I have never killed a man: I am different already
from my stock. I've yet to kill a chicken either,
though I will admit to dissected rats, a rash
of crabs. Hopes and plans people stowed in me.

You wanted me special, and I thank you for that
hope: you meant well, I know. Yet the urge
to migrate early and hard – you gave me that,
through blood and egg. Sometimes I wrote home.

You didn't make me queer: I could have bred.
I sometimes daydream the many wondrous ways
I could have damaged imagined lives. I'm glad
I missed that boat; you had sufficient grief.

I cannot edit my ribose manual. Instead I choose
to cut and mix our stories – yours and mine and all
who went before, wash and stitch us fresh – here:
an Armada of pretty, petty, permanent truths.
