Dad killed a man, once. He was proud of his lack
of accidents until the day a dog jumped from a basket
on a bicycle pedalled by an old farm hand who swerved
into the path of deadly tyres. He cried for a week.

I have never killed a man: I am different already
from my fleet. Though the cargo I carry deep in my hold
was packed in cells and loaded by people I love, I found
other, stranger charts to explore. I found my own storms.

You wanted me better, mum, and I thank you for that
hope: you meant well, I know. Yet the urge to migrate
early and hard – you gave me that too, through blood
and egg: a familiar tradition. Sometimes I wrote home.

There's boats in the Channel, some on the horizon,
some beached here in Dymchurch, some in the dunes
at Greatstone, some ripped on Dungeness shingle.
Who am I to question the silhouettes of strangers?
