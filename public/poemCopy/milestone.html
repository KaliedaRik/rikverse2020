I come home from school to find the party
waiting to surprise me: brother, brother,
sister, mother. The day of keys, with cake.

I have eighteen summers to my bones,
each a band around marrow, now a ring
for my finger. I am an adult, today.

This day, two years before, I earned a gift:
a medal of sorts. A travelling saint's image
to guard my future steps, hung on thin chain.

I was already lost: could they not see,
my brothers and my sister, my mother?
The room was closer, then: cold and tight.

A tin of beer to baptise my new-ringed
finger. A sovereign soldered to gold plated
base metal; a wage of sorts. An insurance.

My family fractured months before I earned
my sixteenth summer. My father fractured
eight full moons ago and I am to be strong.

I was born a dragon, though this is not
a dragon's year. These shortening days
are ruled by dogs, and sovereign St George.

I am a man now, though a judge made me
an adult a month after I put on a necklace.
I must finish my legal beer, and drink more.

Is this it, mum? Is this the sum of me?
