(After Μέρες του 1896 by Κωνσταντίνος Καβάφης)

His humiliation was complete. His interests in love
both immoral and despised
(though natural) was the cause:
society was so curt.
He gradually lost his little money;
following in turn, his reputation.
He was nearing thirty with not a year
of work to his name, as far as was known.
At times he had cash earned from
exchanges that earned shame.
A lad become a man whom, if you were seen with him
too often, well – the rumours would seep.

It's not that, though. It doesn't sit right.
It deserves more, that memory of his beauty.
There's another consideration which, when considered –
it seems, worthy: it seems, simple and genuine –
a child within love, beyond honour
and position posed without question
from pure flesh from pure pleasure.

His face was failed? But it is society
which seems so curt that sees only face.
