November and the mist persists. I have lived
in silence for an age, now, since the judge
summoned you both to London, to that myth
of a Court to order you mute: tell the boy
nothing of the course of your fractures;
let him live each day in a bliss of quiet
ignorance. No treats or threats shall pass
your lips; contempt shall be an invitation
to sit in a room with bars for friends.

November drizzle and a damp of endings.
I don't know why you're angry at me. Dad
has already shared the story the posh woman
with her chunky clank of wooden beads
around her neck has written about me.
I was not a hero to her. I was a victim
too timid to choose between my guardians,
too infant to know the nuance of love.
Dad told me it proved I was soft in limb
and soul. Her fault, he said: she built us
this way – is that why your tears are hot?

November and the air chills. A divorce
has moved into my home. It brings a fresh
quarrel in its bags: who shall move out?
"They made you an adult," you tell me as I
stand tall in my strong mint of sixteen years.
"You're not their Ward anymore. You guard you
now, and I have to give up my money to you."
Is that why you weep, mum? It's just coins,
a weekly dole for my clothes, my books.
If I refuse the cash, will it fix you?
