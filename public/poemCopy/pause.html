I'm sitting on a bench that looks over the canal.
There's a strong heat in the air; the sun is raising
sweat from my skin that fails to cool me to calm.
I'm watching butterflies as they hunt for a sweat
they can drink: two are coupling; one dies in the beak
of a swallow who scissors a tail as it flies by my face.
I'm considering the risks of lighting another cigarette.
I'm sitting on a bench. I'm not thinking of the need
to move forward, to walk back to the road, to dodge
the busy traffic, to step up the hill and through the door
to start the work of greetings and smiles and easy chat.

I went over to the canal. Before I came here.
It's hot out today: is that window wide enough?
How have you been? Where do you ache today?
No, it's still July. Yes, I remember you told me
how your mum would send you away to the station
with enough change to take you down the line
to visit your uncle for six weeks. She never
gave you a bag, just a sandwich. I saw a swallow
today, hunting butterflies. There's lots of buddleia
in bloom by the canal. It's very hot: are you sure
you don't want a fan? Perhaps I can blow on your face.

I'm sitting on a bench that looks over the canal.
There's a strong chill in the air. I slip the jacket
from my arms to remind my skin of the joy of sense.
I want my butterflies back, but they've all gone south
to the comforts of Africa in the bellies of fledged chicks.
I like the way your stories changed as the months passed,
how the names of the players swapped. I can edit away
the way your eyes screwed at the frustration of losing
your next word. Time was slipping its frames for you
and you spited me for it, the way – he – would appear
in my face – no: look! The buddleia still has a flower …
