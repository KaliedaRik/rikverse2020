Have you heard the one about the four brothers?
See, there's four brothers walked into a bar
and each had a corner populated by friends
and gossip and sometimes they'd meet when buying
their next round and they followed pub rules
and chat to fellow thirsters while waiting
to order and, yeah, not much of a joke.

Mum only counted sons, see. "There's only room
for one woman in the kitchen." Those are the words
my sister remembered, flat on her back in bed
with her broken hip and her refusal to give
the radiotherapy another chance to knock
the cancer out of her spine. It's a legacy,
I suppose. Mum could be a funny old stick at times.

I don't know where our humour comes from. My mum
and my dad both came from scary families, histories
fit to rip the faces from social workers. Educated
tones still leave me fidgeting, though the cut
of a privately tutored vowel finds me chilled
and fisted. Not that we swore under the family eaves:
our fights were through jokes and put-outs. Leave
the heavy battles to old man and his missus, sneak out
with just enough cash to buy the next round, see.

So, yeah. I shared my bedroom with brothers, one
for twenty odd years before I sneaked out to London.
He died too young. One down, three to go
said mum.
