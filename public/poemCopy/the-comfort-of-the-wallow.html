When I last edited me, I suffered.
My canon collapsed at the joints,
flesh in the bathroom. I mopped
at the mess for months: I moved;
I hid in a bedsit wallow, alone.

It is an interesting theology,
she says. Today we meet in mist
on the damp edge of the Marshes.
It is a cold sweat around me; leaves
stick to my boots - a kind blanket.

I want to be a clever puzzle,
I admit, with pieces that clip at
each other in a drift of designs,
like deep-rooted cratons testing
my sediments, reshaping continents.

Are five deaths enough to define
the limits of you, she asks? No,
I whisper. I reach down to wipe
leaves from a boot, touch damp slime.
I need more me to weave a good nest.

So, listen: ducks at play on the canal,
dogs leash-strained to chase them.
Not every shape in the fog has a knife:
Some could carry sticks, maybe words?
A shared wallow can be a good puzzle?
