When I last edited me, I suffered.
My canon collapsed at the joints,
flesh in the bathroom. I mopped
at the mess for months: I moved,
I hid in a room in Islington.

It is an interesting theology,
she says. Today we meet in mist
on the crisp edge of the Marshes.
It is a cold sweat around me;
it is a kind blanket of leaves.

I want to be a clever puzzle,
I admit. Pieces that clip into
each other, unforced, in new ways.
I have cratons within me, deep
in their roots, to build continents.

Are five deaths enough to define
the limits of you, she asks? No,
I whisper, the fallen leaves
in my hand limp at their stalks.
I need more to weave a good nest.

No, listen: ducks at play on the canal,
dogs leash-strained to chase them.
Not every shape in the fog has a knife:
some could carry smiles, maybe words?
A shared wallow can make a good nest.
