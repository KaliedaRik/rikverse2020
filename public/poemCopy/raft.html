Knock, knock! No wind, no cloud
in this half-moon sky. Stars
smudge into ripples: knock!

A raft of drift trunks and tyres
and plastic farm wrappers,
half-afloat, a wash at the stern.

"Is anyone there?" No, nothing
here, no breath, just my ghosts.

The mast is me, the sail my shirt.
I have knots for eyes tonight.
"Is anyone there?" Knock, knock!

Two babies snuggle at my boots,
one blue, one tiny. One I shared
a cot with, before we could crawl.

I have bricks to tie to their feet
though casting them overboard
into brine is not much of a lure.

"Is anyone there?" No, nothing here,
no catch to sell on the beach.

You need to work harder, I tell
the younger worm. I have a need
to consult your sister, my mother.

I have questions to ask, I tell
the blue worm. She won't come to
the mirror, I've no more candles.

My raft of jetsam trinkets
leaks: my boots are in brine.
Knock, knock! I heel. Knock?

"Is anyone there?" No, nothing here
in my deep channels, says the water.

Stars smudge in the phosphor glints
of rippled scum that chops as I turn
to catch any breeze in my shirt.

But no wind, tonight. No cloud
to sheet the dry sky, no answers
to questions I already forgot.
