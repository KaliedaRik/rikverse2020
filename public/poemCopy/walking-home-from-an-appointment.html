I always feel the urge to eat
healthier, as I walk back home
from my annual check-in to meet
with doctors, nurses: are you okay?
I suppose the passing drive to do
more me is guilt, a squeeze of shame:
I'll assign blame later, I decide.

The day presents a touch warmer
than expected; the world has a fever
and we need to be on bedside watch.
Typical November leaves spin from twigs:
each sheet a mottled red, jaundice vein.
Children rush in railed school playgrounds,
guided and guarded by tabard stewards.

I've changed, I wanted to tell my medic.
I diminish as fresh events fold about me,
their unasked hug of unfenced facts a wonder
I stroke, pet as they swirl my head.
I do want to be a better shape of me;
I enjoy jolts of chill air in my lungs,
my step step dance of maybe may not time.
