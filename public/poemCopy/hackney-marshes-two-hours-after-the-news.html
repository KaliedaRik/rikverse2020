Pattern this: a grief that wounds
by new paths. You sit on a bench
and watch leaves stretch over sky
but they offer no message. They flag
snapshots in flat shapes, no script
in their fat-vein translucence.

You watch birds skim the water
of the slow flow river. You ask
them if the taste of midges sates
their hunger. You amble through crowds
of people who jog through dirt, cycle
on frames: does the wheel turn? Does
the dragon break from its nymph and know
the struts of what it becomes?

There's a brown haze of grass across
the faint lines of the football pitches,
fallow for the summer. So much activity
halted by this sudden season of baked heat
and flowers, their petals brown as seeds
swell where once the bee sipped. Pattern

this new grief and value its shock
as it shakes thought built on scent
caught on memory of a face that once
held flesh but speaks no more. You walk
where people fought scant weeks before
to score, to win - what? There's air
in my lungs and it wheezes as I push it
out, pull it in, hold it for seconds
as I rehearse an end as dry as sunshine

but it is not my time, you see. This time
is for me to place the fat shape of what
once was, to learn that silence
holds too much.
