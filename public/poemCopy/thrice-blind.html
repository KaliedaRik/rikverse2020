My mother's house is haunted: they're cold,
she told me one late spring afternoon,
but not scary. They just push and push
and stop me walking along the hall.

My sister never liked that house: they hung
dad's clock in the living room and it stops
wrong, she told me one rainy autumn evening
in a backroad bar in London. It's your round.

All I can think of is a herd of cows moving
through a field long gone, looking for clover,
or perhaps winter hay. Dad used to kill cows
at the abattoir, I told her, feeling for notes.

I never felt comfortable, there in that house,
though I'm thrice blind: stars; faces; ghosts.
There was something about the way the mum-dad
rows echoed, or maybe the locked up shotguns.
