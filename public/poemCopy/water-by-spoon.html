A hot breeze, today, grit in its teeth
as it gnaws at the cracked-open window.

Still air, between palms that almost grasp,
between faces, my words lipped in smiles.

This room is too big, its bed hiding
in a corner, chairs tight by its side.

A cat hangs on a wall where it can be seen;
the real cat curls in your chair at home.

Pots of small plants grown leggy on a sill,
each an impulse gift to mark a fleet visit.

You summoned me; I am obedient. I am heavy
with a shirt soaked in grit and refusals.

A cup of warm water; a teaspoon. I am not
trained to lift spoons to cracked lips.

You summoned me and we both know this
is the last visit, the last planted gift.

I refuse to gift this stupidity my tears;
"I'll always be sat on your hip, mum."

You want to touch me, cradle my cheek
in your palm and I will not refuse you this.

Between sips, a thin smile. How can eyes
say at once: I hurt; I am not scared; I love?

I break another rule, take the spoon, dip it
in water and lift it to lips: sip us, mum.
