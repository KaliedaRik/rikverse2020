What if this grief is my pupa?
This skin does feel hard, like
nails grown to hooves, sheaths.

Now I am changing, how shall I
build? I could lie on my side
and flounder, shift eyes to my cheek.
Flat, I can eat shoals, or dirt.

What if I'm nested? What if
my story is to shed endless shells:
grasshopper, or locust aswarmed?

Do I need hands, mum? They ask
too much from me; they fiddle
with ribbons and writing. They pick
at scabs, sometimes not mine.

I don't need wings, you can keep
yours: they dazzle, confuse me
like spice in your Christmas curry.

I have been feeding on life
for decades, a cabbage of holes now
which I find pretty. But I desire
to vomit silk, I feel: hang; change.

Who will eat me as I dangle, still?
I have poisons to dissolve flesh.
I am not an easy meal to digest.
