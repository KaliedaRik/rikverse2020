Know this: it was the death of sunlight and sky that drew me to this fresh rye distill. Copper clouds drew reasons to thoughts, and muscles don't obey my orders in such moods. I've been gripping the neck for a long while now, watching stars flick between heavens.

I wish for rain: can you smell the drops? They reek of cool transactions and the promise of ice in tumblers.

Each sputter appears like a revelation on the pane that separates the beyond from here. Dark they are, a spit of harsh muse laughing at the hope of bottled comforts, thoughtlessly sipped. The first drop was bronzed jaundice, I remember now, a prismatic gathering of this bastard city's urgent towers and spires into a single daylit farewell.

Can you see the drop on the pane sag? There's enough gathered now to rivulet a course through dirt. They witness me as I watch them stipple a braille poem across glass. The only word I can read is: fool!

Anj ain't got no time for drunks, not tonight. Before she leaves our heap of space that pretends to be our office, her blouse two sizes too tight and her skirt a pencil of innuendoes, she clears my garbage of empties – ten cents on returns. She slaps my cheeks for luck and marks my brow with a kiss.

Such a night lensed in the rain. I wish I could walk through the fresh chill of washed air, my sniper's guile wrapping mysteries around my flesh. I wish – I wish I could stand…
