I ask Diogenes: "is it enough
to spit on graves?" The man before me holds
a rough-wrought bowl of scavenged lentil broth;
he sips at it direct, not caring to fold
its thickened juice in bread. "You look to be
a person rich in spit," he says. "Your boots
appear to lack the mud of one who claims
to dig such graves; perhaps you seek pursuits
to fill these holes that you despise? Or would
you lie in one yourself? I'd rather spend
my spit on tyrants, drunkards, those who wound
the world for glory, passion, spite, my friend."
