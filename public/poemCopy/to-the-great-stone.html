I do not need to atone, I tell the rock
stacked in this field we share. I have lived
life and others have cashed their time through me
and now they are gone, I say, and I can sit here
in the September mud like you, our multi-ton weights
caught in the autumn clippings of grass and leaves.
Yet also, I consider, I can stand and shake away
the rock climbers who practice their craft across
my face, but you must endure the limpet athletes
for that, dear rock, is your one purpose and you
are a shape quarried much as I, too, was made.
But I have legs, a will to step elsewhere
and maybe forgive those who traipse
around me and chip at my skin.
And though you may remain
plain to see for millennia
and I will be gone
to dust, who
between us is
stronger?

A late butterfly sips at an overdue spray
of mauve budleia that pokes from the root
of the rounded boulder. The flower is thin
and long, uncultivated. The insect keeps
its wings tight and bland as it drinks
the mixed mead from the soft cup.

Shall I crush them, the rock asks?
You can read my history in crystals.
I still feel the heat of the earth
in my veins. I do not need to atone
for what I am become. We do not need
to trade offers of forgiveness.

Raindrops across
my rough, gray surface:
I would like to apologise
for each tear my finger
flicks from a cheek.
