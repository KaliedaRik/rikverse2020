When I first crawled I crawled first
to the nest in the corner of the room
where we ate, where I drew first breath.
Here I found dog: short fur, long tongue;
black and very male. Before words I found
a world of tooth nip, ear twitch, scratch
and yelp snort, stretch and snout tilt.
We curled in our den, dog and I, settled
into my sodden nappy, his roadkill attar.

Dog died a while after I stood on my shanks.
He did not leave; he never arrived. Still
he sits on the edge of things, just beyond.

He has kept station by my mother this last year,
since she took to her bed and refused to take
more steps; as her skin festered, hid in wraps
of bandages tucked under sheets. He curled
by the piss bag, in shadows, our last day.
All is good, he told me; I can lead her on.
I am the opening and the way. I cocked ear,
with kind eye: I know, Anub. I always knew.
