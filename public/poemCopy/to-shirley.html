By the time you reached my vexed age
you had survived two cardiac impacts.
You reported no visions: if they waited
for you beyond, they kept their distance.

We dabbled in occults, an age back. We read
birthdays in stars, sometimes bestiaries.
You even bought me books for my birthdays
on how to read decks or the fall of sticks.

I wanted to hunt derelictions for ghosts;
you consumed your shivers through televisions.
You did not need the dust of abandoned bricks:
we grew up in houses we both chose to run from.

You fell in the street and broke your hip
the day before our mother stopped sipping
at air. Your son didn't make it to the funeral;
he never woke up in time, another heart failed.

Strange, the way fate throws coincidence
in our faces like an annoying street mime.
Thank you for dying a week before my birthday.
Thank you for saning beside me through hard years.
