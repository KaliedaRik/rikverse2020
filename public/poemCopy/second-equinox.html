I would bargain with death, I tell her. She offers
a smile of straight lines: you must find death?
Yes, I agree, reaching down to pluck the head
of a michaelmas daisy from its stem: here it is.

The log on which we sit, astride, is damp. The mist
so common to the season has lifted late today.
I wear unsuitable cotton – thin, baggy, tethered
with twisted rope. She wears spring in her eyes.

I would bargain with death to distract it. The lines
of her lips arch, up and down, a sincere pout.
The flower is not dead yet, she says. You can float
it in water and watch it set seeds, if you like.

Am I not a seed already? I know where the valley is,
the one with pyres along the ridges and the flames
of sconced torches lining the path. I would go there
to discuss terms and conditions with finality.

I can feel the heat on my skin already. The sky
leaks sapphire through marble. The sun burns
leaves to russets and nets. Twigs fatten their tips
and all the wind carries is her sly-soft laugh.
