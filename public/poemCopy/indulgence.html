That man, sat in the front pew, quiet and intent
as the music plays, as the pall bearers settle
the coffin atop the slab – yes, that indulgence
of a son – he needs to rage. He needs to take
an axe to the wood. He needs to reach in and prise
his mother upright. He needs to scream in her
wax face and deny her state, deny her the aged
gift to wither in her bed and now boil in flame.
That man with a maze in his skull demands
the vault above to explode, to make radiance
flood across this tasteful room, drag the angels
from heaven to explain, in simple words scribed
in no book, no hymn, why God created pain, loss,
hurt. He wants the celestial choir to choke
on their trumpets, watch their immanence ignite
the oxygen in the air. That lost man, him, he
fidgets for answers beyond the lyric, the music.
He wants, needs, a tear to drip from his eye.
He aches to unroll his mask, touch folds
of grief in his face. And the pall bearers
bow at their suited waists to honour
the ceremony. And cut flowers settle
on the lid: "Our Father, who art …"
