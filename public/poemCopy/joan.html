She hides herself in curtains. There's a ruck
of demons trapped in fractured air; they slick
at her skin. She keeps her charms close and ticks
her thumb on each: a shell; a feather plucked
from a crow, dead in the street's gutter. Why
does Hell hide in the eyes of strangers? When
did Satan settle on Joan's soul? But then
she knows there's evil everywhere: the sigh
of doors; a light-switch click; the quiet hymn
of unseen codes that cut across the cloud
and slit her hopes, her thoughts – she grips her shroud
of curtains, summons fibs and pseudonyms
and prays to silent saints that don't exist
to stop the skirl of pulsing words …
