It's a time lapse, this hand. Watch how
the nail forms and spreads across the top
of the tip; observe how the knuckle knots
and bends. This is a human hand – the thumb
can circle against fingers: soon enough
it pinches at worlds, plucks through visions.
Across the hand's back, veins; across the palm
a web of creases, lines that offer futures.

One future is the hands that take this hand
and pull it upwards, out from the cot.
Is that how you saw me grow? All I recall
is holding my hand ahead of my eyes, daring it
to sprout more fingers, reach for the sun.

My soil was shallow, my roots loose. When
I dispersed, I kept my distance, for reasons
we discussed only in pheromones and strokes.

You sit on my shelf now: I have no rush
to return you to the sod of Kent chalk.
You've germinated gardens in me already;
I hear ghosts whisper in the scurry, at night.
