They built a new railway, from me to you. They said
it was for fast trains to France; I knew the truth.

From Stratford to the Kent shore is an hour on the train.
Enough journey time to feel you ease back into my bones.

Cold, cold that wind across your flat fields, and hot
steam drawn by the high sun from your tight ditches.

Nobody knows me in London, which is an embrace. You add
an aura of familiar fear to my face: danger; beware.

I visit you in dreams, or you come visit for a moment
to remind me: I can run, but your everwind is mine.

And sheep on green, and butter rape, and the tractor
evicting potatoes and me to pick them into sacks.

I never got into fights, growing up. I never fight
now; I keep my fists and heels safe. I have words.

There's a sound in my ears, a sussur of wave over
pebble, a wind across flat, hedged fields. Tinnitus.
