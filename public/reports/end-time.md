## Academic Review: Key Themes, Critical Analysis, and Research Contributions

Rik Roots’s "End Time" is a thematically ambitious poetry collection that marries the diction and narrative framing of hardboiled detective fiction with theological allegory, speculative futurism, and poetic postmodernism. The text comprises a sequence of linked poems that follow a disillusioned investigator through a surreal, terminal cityscape. Along the way, it engages with classical and biblical intertexts, post-industrial ruin, and the unstable role of the self-as-narrator in times of collapse.

### Key Themes and Intertextual Dynamics:

* **Post-Apocalyptic Urbanism:** The poems take place in a dystopian metropolis marked by decay, religious confusion, and violent entropy. This imagined city becomes a stand-in for late-capitalist collapse, echoing themes familiar from noir fiction but refracted through a mythopoeic lens.

* **Divine Absence and Religious Echoes:** Throughout, motifs from Abrahamic scripture are subverted or recontextualized. Messianic figures recur — some recognizable, others fragmented — and are variously betrayed, mourned, or interrogated. Theological symbolism ("holes in the hands," "he screamed blood from his eyes," "he won’t scream no more for this dead making") functions not as moral guideposts but as unreliable artefacts in a decayed moral universe.

* **Detective Fiction as Allegorical Frame:** The central narrator adopts the voice and posture of a gumshoe PI, allowing the poems to explore existential inquiry through the formal clichés of noir — femme fatales, cryptic clients, brooding city nights, and a relentless search for a "truth" that is unstable or absent. The noir idiom is both homage and critique, ultimately dismantled by the poetic form.

* **Fragmented Identity and Memory:** The poems resist linearity, instead presenting memories and confessions as disjointed vignettes. This reflects the epistemological instability of the world portrayed - a place where mirrors lie, histories are overwritten, and language itself may be unreliable.

* **Poetic Form and Intertextual Experimentation:** Many of the poems experiment with formal repetition, polyphony, and metafiction (e.g., "Death of a Poem"), pushing the boundary between poem and narrative prose. References to classical mythology, the Book of Revelation, and pulp detective stories surface throughout, creating a richly layered textual matrix.

### Critical Context and Contributions:

* "End Time" contributes to ongoing scholarly discussions around **genre hybridity in contemporary poetry**, particularly the merging of speculative fiction and poetic form.

* It engages with **post-secular poetics**, offering a deeply ambivalent treatment of religious themes that reflects the uncertainty of faith in the Anthropocene.

* Its use of detective fiction tropes as a poetic structure allows for a **literary interrogation of knowledge, culpability, and redemption**, inviting comparison with authors such as Paul Auster (City of Glass) and Anne Carson.

* The chapbook also offers a case study in **voice construction and narrative fragmentation**, of interest to scholars exploring lyric subjectivity in postmodern and post-apocalyptic literature.
